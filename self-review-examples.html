How to write a self-review.

A self-review is the one document in your year where you tell the story of your work before someone else does. Keep it short. Make it specific, and true.

The shape that works

  • Impact. What changed because of your work, for users, the team or the business. Before and after.
  • Collaboration. How you made other people faster or better, with one example each.
  • Growth. What you learned, what you'd do differently, and what you want to own next.

Three to five bullets in each. One page is plenty; your manager reads a stack of these in the same week.

Impact • Brought p95 latency of the recipe search API from 820 ms to 310 ms (APP-418). Recipe App 2.0 launched on the faster version. Collaboration • Wrote the sync API contract with Neha before any server code, so mobile started two weeks early. Growth • Next half: own capacity planning for sync traffic.

An example of the shape. The project and numbers are made up.

Five rules

  1. Evidence first. Gather it before you write. You'll remember the last month and forget the first five.
  2. Before and after. "From 820 ms to 310 ms" beats "much faster". Without a number, describe a change someone could see.
  3. Your part, clearly. "I led", "I wrote" and "I decided" for your part, "we" for the team's. Don't borrow in either direction.
  4. Admit one miss. A line on what didn't work, and what you changed, reads as judgement.
  5. Point forward. End with what you want to own next half. It turns the review into a plan.

Phrases to drop

"Worked on", "helped with", "was involved in", "various", "ensured quality", "drove alignment". They say you were near the work. Replace each one with what you did and what changed.
