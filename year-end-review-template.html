Template · Year-end review

Year-end review template for developers.

The year-end review is the one where memory fails hardest. You remember the last six weeks in detail and January as a blur, so the review ends up describing November.

The fix is to start from evidence. Rebuild the year from git first, then fill in the template below. Thirty minutes is enough.

Step 1: rebuild your year from git (1 minute)

Run these in each repository you worked in this year. They read commit messages and dates only.

Every commit you made this year, oldest first:

git log --since="2026-01-01" --author="$(git config user.email)" --no-merges --reverse --date=short --pretty=format:"%ad %s"

How many commits per month, to spot your busiest stretches:

git log --since="2026-01-01" --author="$(git config user.email)" --no-merges --date=format:"%Y-%m" --pretty=format:"%ad" | sort | uniq -c

Then add what git doesn't see: closed tickets, design docs, incidents, interviews, onboarding and reviews you gave. A brag doc makes this step trivial next year.

Step 2: the template (copy it)

Year in review · [your name] · [team] · 2026 Headline results (3–5, strongest first) • [what you did] — [before → after] — [link] Impact on the team and the product • [project or area] — [what changed for users or the team] Helping others • [who] — [how: reviews, pairing, onboarding, unblocking] — [what it led to] Growth • [skill or system you learned] — [where you used it] Didn't go to plan • [what happened] — [what you changed afterwards] Next year • [1–3 goals you want your manager's help with]

A filled-in example

A year for a mobile engineer, cut down to one page. The project, people and numbers are made up.

Year in review · iOS engineer · Recipe App team · 2026 Headline results • Shipped offline sync for Recipe App 2.0 — failed saves 4.1% → 0.6% — SYNC-142 • Cut CI build time 22 → 9 minutes by caching dependencies • Fixed the launch crash on older devices — crash-free users 99.1% → 99.7% Impact on the team and the product • Sync SDK — moved three apps onto one sync layer; new screens now take days, not weeks Helping others • Neha — paired through her first on-call week; she ran the next incident on her own • Reviewed 240+ pull requests, mostly Sync SDK Growth • Learned staged rollouts — used them for every sync change since August Didn't go to plan • The first sync rollout went to everyone at once; after two crash reports I moved us to staged rollouts Next year • Lead the Sync SDK 3.0 design with Ravi • Mentor one new engineer through their first launch

Step 3: write it in 30 minutes

  1. 10 minutes: list. From the git log and your tickets, write one line per thing you shipped or fixed. Don't judge yet.
  2. 10 minutes: pick and measure. Choose the 3–5 lines that changed the most. Add the before and after for each; a dashboard, the ticket or the PR description usually has it.
  3. 5 minutes: the people. Add who you helped and how. It's the part of the year that leaves the least trace in tools.
  4. 5 minutes: honest and forward. One line that didn't go to plan, and up to three goals for next year.

Want the lines sorted for you? Paste your list into the free self-review generator. It groups lines into impact, collaboration and growth and marks every line still missing a number. For what reviewers look for in your role, see self-review examples for 10 roles.

What to leave out

Meetings you attended, work that isn't finished, and anything you'd be uncomfortable reading aloud to the person it's about. Replace "worked on" and "involved in" with what you did and what changed.
